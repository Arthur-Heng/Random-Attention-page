Random Attention

Rethinking KV Cache Eviction for Efficient Reasoning

Heng Wang1,2, Jielin Qiu1, Wenting Zhao1, Cheng Qian1,2, Liangwei Yang1, Jiawei Han2, Heng Ji2, Silvio Savarese1, Shelby Heinecke1, Huan Wang1

1Salesforce AI Research · 2University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign

📄 arXiv (soon) ⭐ Code BibTeX
TL;DR — Every KV cache eviction method scores cached tokens and keeps the top-K. We replace the score with a coin flip: keep the prompt, evict the rest uniformly at random within each attention head.
  • Across four reasoning models and six tasks, it matches the strongest prior evictor — and is significantly ahead in 31 of 60 main-table comparisons, behind in one.
  • It never runs a scoring pass, so in vLLM serving it delivers 32–43% more tokens per second than the strongest baseline at 32k-token generations.
  • Why it works: the prompt is the fragile part of the cache, and the reasoning trace protects itself — restated in the text, copied across heads.

The result

A coin flip matches five generations of selection signals

We evaluate at a roughly 4×-compressed cache budget on MATH500, GPQA-Diamond, AIME 2025, AIME 2026, HMMT and LiveCodeBench, on Qwen3-4B/14B/32B and Phi-4-reasoning, with matched budgets and paired significance tests throughout. Random Attention matches the strongest prior evictor on every model, is significantly ahead of a baseline in 31 of the 60 main-table comparisons, and is significantly behind in exactly one (code reasoning on Qwen3-32B, explained below).

Mechanism I

The prompt is the fragile part of the cache

Prior evictors differ in whether the question survives: some pin the prompt by rule, others let their score decide. When we give every method the same rule — keep the prompt, score only the generated trace — most of the gap between them disappears, and each method gains almost exactly as much as its score had been losing of the question. SnapKV, the weakest unprotected baseline, gains up to +35 accuracy points from prompt protection alone.

What separates eviction methods is mostly what they protect, not how they rank the rest.

Mechanism II

The reasoning trace protects itself

Why does random eviction of the trace not hurt? Because the trace is stored redundantly at two levels. In the text: the model restates the intermediates it still needs as it works, so a needed value usually has a recent copy. Across attention heads: every head holds its own copy of every token, and eviction decides per head which copies die. A planted-fact probe makes the second level visible: a fact surviving in a single KV head is almost never retrieved (3% of the time), while the same fact surviving in several heads is retrieved almost always (99% with all heads) — and the shape of the surviving copies barely matters.

The boundary

Where a selection signal still matters

The redundancy argument predicts its own failure modes, and we map them. Push compression from 4× to 16× and the regime flips: on MATH500 Random Attention collapses with the context while value-based selection still holds usable accuracy. And on code reasoning, prompts are so long that protecting them consumes most of the budget — the one setting where a baseline (TriAttention) is significantly ahead. Selection earns its cost exactly where rehearsal and redundancy run out: extreme budgets, and the rare fact stated once and never restated.

Serving

No scoring pass means real speed

Random Attention needs no calibration, no tuning, and no per-step scoring. Integrated into vLLM, it serves 32–43% more tokens per second than the strongest baseline at 32k-token generations, and up to 28.8× the throughput of full attention at the tightest budget (K=1024) — while matching the baseline's accuracy. It is also the baseline any new selection signal has to beat, at matched budget and matched prompt protection.

Use it

Try it on your own model

The repository contains the eviction engine (every method in the paper as an eviction mode), the evaluation harness, the paired significance tests, the efficiency benchmarks and the vLLM port.

git clone https://github.com/SalesforceAIResearch/Random-Attention && cd Random-Attention
bash setup.sh && . env.sh

# one (model, task, method, budget) accuracy cell
scripts/run_cell.sh Qwen3-4B math random_pp   # Random Attention, K defaults to the ~4x point
scripts/run_cell.sh Qwen3-4B math triattn     # strongest baseline, per-model calibration
scripts/grade_cell.sh Qwen3-4B math
